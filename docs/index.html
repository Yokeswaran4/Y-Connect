Features

Everything you reach for, one tap away

Clipboard sync

Copy on your computer and paste on your phone. Android to desktop works from a Quick Settings tile, the Share menu or an optional automatic mode.

Android to desktop works from the Quick Settings tile, the Share menu, or an optional automatic mode (that one needs “Display over other apps”). On Wayland, wl-clipboard is used when it is installed.

More

Notifications

Mirror Android notifications to your desktop. Linux desktop notifications can be forwarded too when dbus-monitor is installed.

Android notifications arrive on your desktop with the app’s name and icon where possible. Forwarding Linux desktop notifications needs dbus-monitor. Their text is never put into debug reports.

More

Files and folders

Send anything in either direction with progress, cancel, safe filenames and automatic resume after a dropped connection.

Folders are sent too. Every transfer can be cancelled, filenames are made safe, and an interrupted transfer continues on its own or with Resume. Received files go to Downloads/YConnect. Data travels in large encrypted chunks and is buffered on the receiving side, so big files move at close to your network’s speed.

More

Screen sharing

Show your computer’s screen on your phone, or your phone’s screen on your computer. View only, over the same encrypted link.

Press Share screen on a device card (Screen on Android) and the other device opens a live view. Android asks for screen-capture permission every time and shows a notification with a Stop button while it shares. Closing the viewer, or pressing Stop, ends the session. The picture is compressed, kept to about 15 frames per second, and paused while the connection is busy so file transfers aren’t slowed. On macOS allow Screen Recording; on Wayland pick the screen in the system dialog.

More

Auto discovery

Devices find each other over UDP. On networks that block it, use Connect by IP.

Devices announce themselves on UDP 48999 and connect over TCP 48998. Guest Wi‑Fi or router isolation can block discovery, so Connect by IP is always there as a fallback.

More

Battery and latency

See each device’s battery level and charging state, plus live latency, jitter and packet loss on the encrypted link.

Latency is measured through the encrypted link, every 2 seconds while the app is open. Tap a device to see a graph with average, best, worst, jitter and packet loss.

More

Remote terminal and commands

Open a live terminal on your Linux or Windows PC from your phone, or run one-off commands. Off by default, never elevated, with delete and sudo guard rails.

Commands run as your normal user and stop after at most 2 minutes. Deleting files and sudo are always refused, and only phones paired with your code can ask.

Questions

Quick answers

Does Y Connect need the internet?

No. Devices talk directly over your Wi‑Fi or LAN, with no cloud and no account. The one optional exception is checking for new releases: it asks GitHub for the latest version, sends nothing about you or your devices, and is off by default in the apps.

How do I find out about new versions?

On Android open About → Updates, on Linux and Windows open Settings → About & updates. Press Check now, or switch on Notify me about new releases to be told about once a day. You can also use the version checker in the Download section on this page.

Is my data encrypted?

Yes. Messages and file data use AES‑256‑GCM, with a key derived from a random pairing code created for each pair of devices using PBKDF2‑SHA256 (100,000 iterations). The code is handed over once, encrypted, when you tap Accept.

My phone doesn’t show up. What now?

Make sure both devices are on the same network and that guest Wi‑Fi or router isolation isn’t blocking UDP discovery. Then use Connect by IP. You may need to allow TCP 48998 and UDP 48999 in your firewall.

Why does the Android app ask for battery and notification access?

Notification access lets it forward notifications to your computer. Setting battery to Unrestricted stops Android from shutting down the background link.

How does screen sharing work, and can someone watch without me knowing?

No. Sharing only starts when the person whose screen it is presses Share screen (or Screen on Android, which also shows Android’s own permission prompt every time and a notification while it runs). The other device only watches: it can’t click or type. It works in both directions between paired devices and ends when either side closes it or the connection drops.

Can the phone control my computer?

Only if you turn on Allow commands from phone on the computer. It’s off by default, commands never run with administrator rights, and deleting files is always refused.
